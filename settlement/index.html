Settlement-First Direction

SETTLEMENT

What It Means

Settlement-first means outcomes, not only transfers

Payment chains move units from one address to another. A settlement-first chain is built around whether a defined commercial outcome can be expressed, anchored, proved, and resolved under objective rules.

Irium is being positioned around a narrower problem than generic payment chains. The chain is meant to support escrow, agreements, and objective settlement paths where release, refund, or timeout behavior can be tied to predefined rules rather than informal trust.

That only works if the base layer stays serious about what consensus should and should not do. The goal is not to put subjective human judgment on-chain. The goal is to create a mineable public chain where verifiable commercial conditions can anchor to objective state.

What belongs inside the model

Escrow and refundsDefined release and fallback paths matter more than simple one-way transfers for many real transactions.
Agreement anchoringCommercial terms, hashes, and references can be bound to immutable chain state for later verification.
Objective proof pathsTimeouts, signatures, attestations, and deterministic checks are compatible with a narrow consensus model.

What should stay outside consensus

No subjective judgesConsensus should not depend on open-ended human arbitration or protocol-level discretion.
No admin override pathThere should be no privileged key able to freeze, seize, or rewrite expected ownership outcomes.
No vague governance settlementRules-based, verifiable outcomes are the priority, not social-layer intervention inside the protocol.

Why Payments Are Not Enough

Commercial coordination usually requires more than a transfer

Many real transactions need deposits, milestones, refunds, holdbacks, or a response window. That is where settlement infrastructure starts to matter.

A contractor payment is rarely just a transfer. It may need milestone release, delayed holdback, or proof that a defined checkpoint was reached. A preorder or supplier deposit may need a timeout refund if the other side never completes the expected step.

OTC trades also expose the gap between transfer rails and settlement rails. Counterparties often need a safer way to stage value movement than simply trusting the other side to act after payment is sent.

Contractor work: milestones, holdbacks, completion signals, and staged release logic.
Deposits and preorders: refund conditions matter as much as the initial payment.
OTC settlement: peer-to-peer deals benefit from escrow references and objective fallback rules.
Business flows: agreements often need evidence paths and explicit default handling, not just transfer settlement.

How Irium Differs

Built for objective settlement, with a mineable base layer

Irium is not trying to be a generic chain with every possible feature attached to it. It starts from an independent SHA-256d Proof-of-Work chain built from scratch in Rust, then extends toward settlement primitives that remain compatible with objective-only consensus.

The chain is mineable, not a fork, and does not rely on admin keys, freeze controls, or foundation override powers. Mining remains essential because it secures the public base layer that all future settlement functionality depends on.

Base-layer principles

Independent chainRust implementation, independent networking, and SHA-256d Proof-of-Work rather than a forked identity.
Mineable securitySettlement is built on a public miner-secured chain, not on privileged operator control.

Consensus principles

Narrow and objectiveDeterministic validation remains the boundary for consensus behavior.
No override powersNo admin path, no freeze mechanism, and no centralized seizure control embedded in the protocol.

Roadmap Phases

A clearer progression from base layer to commercial infrastructure

The phases below are intentionally separated by status so the page stays honest about what is live, what is under active development, and what remains planned.

Phase 1: Core independent PoW foundation

Live

The live chain already includes the independent SHA-256d base layer, Rust implementation, public mining access, public explorer, public pool, calculator, and core network resilience work such as bootstrap and peer survivability.

Independent chain and mineable Proof-of-Work.
Rust implementation and mainnet-first operating model.
Public explorer, pool, calculator, and transparent founder vesting.

Phase 2: Settlement foundation

In development

This phase is about the basic primitives required for trust-minimized commercial settlement: agreement-hash anchoring, escrow-oriented outputs, timeout refund paths, and simpler flows for OTC escrow, deposits, and contractor-style release logic.

Agreement-hash anchoring and settlement references.
Native escrow and settlement output direction.
Timeout refunds and basic commercial settlement primitives.

Phase 3: Objective proof automation

Planned

This is roadmap work, not a live claim. The goal is proof-triggered settlement through deterministic checks such as approved attestors, signatures, objective evidence paths, and no-response auto-settlement under explicit rules.

Objective proof validation and approved attestor paths.
Signature-verifiable proof flows and no-response auto-settlement.
Milestone proofs and delayed holdback logic.

Phase 4: Commercial infrastructure

Planned

The later layer is broader commercial tooling: templates, contractor and merchant flows, recurring settlement direction, portable reputation tooling, and APIs or integrations that help businesses use the chain more directly.

Commercial templates and repeatable settlement patterns.
Contractor, merchant, and installment-style workflow direction.
Portable reputation and integration APIs as future infrastructure.
