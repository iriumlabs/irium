Settlement-First Blockchain

The blockchain for agreements, not just payments.

Irium (IRM) is a mineable, settlement-first blockchain for escrow, agreements, and proof-based commerce.

The live network is already an independent SHA-256d Proof-of-Work chain built from scratch in Rust. Its broader direction is trust-minimized commercial settlement built around objective rules, verifiable outcomes, and a narrow consensus model with no admin keys or override powers.

What Settlement-First Means

A chain designed around commercial outcomes rather than simple transfers

Payment chains move units from one address to another. A settlement-first chain is designed around whether a commercial outcome can be defined, anchored, proved, and resolved under objective rules. That is the difference between a transfer rail and settlement infrastructure.

Irium is being positioned around a narrower and more practical problem than generic payment chains. The goal is not to put human judgment on-chain. The goal is to support escrow, agreements, refunds, timeouts, and proof-linked release paths where outcomes follow predefined rules rather than informal trust.

That direction only works if the base layer stays disciplined about its boundaries. Consensus should remain narrow, deterministic, and objective-only. Subjective arbitration, governance overrides, freeze controls, and seizure powers do not belong inside the protocol.

What belongs in the model

Agreement anchoringCommercial terms, hashes, and references can bind to immutable chain state for later verification.
Escrow and refundsDefined release and fallback paths matter more than one-way transfers in many real transactions.
Proof-linked settlementTimeouts, signatures, attestations, and deterministic checks fit a narrow consensus model.

Why Payments Are Not Enough

Commercial coordination usually needs more than sending value

Deposits, milestone payments, holdbacks, refunds, response windows, and proof-linked release conditions all sit beyond the scope of a simple transfer. That gap is where settlement infrastructure starts to matter.

OTC tradesPeer-to-peer trading often needs escrow references, release conditions, and fallback paths.
Contractor workMilestone release, delayed holdbacks, and proof of completion are common requirements.
DepositsRefund handling and default logic can matter as much as the original payment itself.
Business flowsCommercial agreements usually need evidence paths and objective release or refund outcomes.

How Irium Differs

Mineable infrastructure first, settlement direction second

Irium is not being presented as a generic chain with every possible feature attached to it. It starts from an independent SHA-256d Proof-of-Work foundation and extends carefully toward settlement primitives that remain compatible with objective-only consensus.

Independent chainIrium is not a fork. It has its own Rust implementation, network stack, and chain identity.
Mineable securityMining remains central because miners secure the public base layer that settlement functionality depends on.
Fair launch profileNo premine, no ICO, no presale, no VC control, and transparent founder vesting where publicly referenced.
Resilient network foundationDNS-free bootstrap, peer discovery resilience, anchor checkpoints, and public infrastructure remain important.

Mining and Verification

The settlement story still starts with the base chain

Mining remains essential because it secures the live network, public explorer, public pool, and the settlement-first infrastructure Irium is building toward. The broader commerce narrative does not replace mining. It gives the base layer a clearer purpose.
