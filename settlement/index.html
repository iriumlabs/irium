Settlement-First Blockchain

The blockchain for agreements, not just payments.

Irium (IRM) is a mineable, settlement-first blockchain for escrow, agreements, and proof-based commerce.

The live network is already an independent SHA-256d Proof-of-Work chain built from scratch in Rust. Its broader direction is trust-minimized commercial settlement with objective rules, verifiable outcomes, and no admin keys or override powers embedded in consensus.

What Settlement-First Means

A chain built around commercial outcomes

Payment chains move value from one address to another. A settlement-first chain is designed around whether a commercial outcome can be defined, anchored, proved, and resolved under objective rules. That is the difference between a transfer rail and a settlement layer.

Irium is being positioned around a narrower and more practical problem than generic payment chains. The aim is not to turn consensus into a court. The aim is to support escrow, agreements, refunds, timeouts, and proof-linked release paths where outcomes are governed by predefined rules rather than informal trust.

That direction only works if the base layer stays serious about its boundaries. Consensus should remain narrow, deterministic, and objective-only. Subjective human arbitration, governance overrides, freeze controls, and seizure powers do not belong inside the protocol.

Inside the modelAgreement-hash anchoring, escrow-oriented outputs, timeout refund logic, signatures, attestations, and deterministic settlement conditions.
Outside the modelOpen-ended human judgment, admin intervention, protocol-level discretion, and centralized dispute authority inside consensus.
Why that mattersTrust-minimized settlement only works if rule enforcement is objective and verifiable rather than dependent on who controls the system.

Why Payments Are Not Enough

Real transactions usually need more than a transfer

Deposits, milestone payments, holdbacks, refunds, response windows, and proof-linked release conditions all sit beyond the scope of a simple transfer. That gap is where settlement infrastructure starts to matter.

01OTC tradingPeer-to-peer trades often need escrow references, release conditions, and fallback paths rather than trust-only coordination.
02Contractor workMilestone release, delayed holdbacks, and proof of completion are common commercial requirements.
03Deposits and preordersTimeout refunds and explicit default handling can matter as much as the original payment itself.
04Business flowsCommercial agreements usually need evidence paths, documented terms, and objective release or refund outcomes.

How Irium Differs

Mineable infrastructure first, settlement direction second

Irium is not being presented as a generic chain with every possible feature attached to it. It starts from an independent SHA-256d PoW foundation and extends carefully toward settlement primitives that remain compatible with objective-only consensus.

Independent chainIrium is not a fork. It has its own Rust implementation, its own network stack, and its own chain identity.
Mineable securityMining remains central because miners secure the public base layer that any future settlement functionality depends on.
Fair launch profileNo premine, no ICO, no presale, no VC control, and transparent founder vesting where referenced publicly.
Resilient network foundationDNS-free bootstrap direction, resilient peer discovery, anchor checkpoints, light-client orientation, and public infrastructure remain important.

Roadmap Phases

Live chain first, broader settlement in phases

The roadmap below separates what is already live from what is under active development and what remains planned. That distinction matters if the settlement narrative is going to stay technically credible.

Phase 1 · Live
Core independent PoW foundation Independent SHA-256d Proof-of-Work, Rust implementation, public explorer, public pool, calculator, fair launch profile, and core network resilience remain the live foundation of the project.
Phase 2 · In Development
Settlement foundation Agreement-hash anchoring, native escrow and settlement output direction, timeout refunds, and simpler flows for OTC escrow, deposits, and contractor release logic.
Phase 3 · Planned
Objective proof automation Objective proof validation, approved attestors, signature-verifiable evidence, no-response auto-settlement, milestone proofs, and delayed holdback paths. This is roadmap work, not a live claim.
Phase 4 · Planned
Commercial infrastructure Templates for business settlement, contractor and merchant flows, recurring invoice or installment direction, portable reputation tooling, and broader integration APIs.

Mining and Verification

The settlement story still starts with the base chain

Mining remains essential because it secures the live network, public explorer, public pool, and the settlement-first infrastructure Irium is building toward. The broader commerce narrative does not replace mining. It gives the base layer a clearer purpose.
